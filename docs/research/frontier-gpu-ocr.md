# Frontier GPU OCR and document parsing models

**Research snapshot:** 2026-11-04
**Purpose:** shortlist evidence for later local evaluation of the eOCR PDF → editable DOCX pipeline. This is a public-source comparison only. No weights were downloaded and no inference or local benchmarks were run.

## Finding

The current evidence supports a *shortlist for controlled testing*, not a model choice. OpenBMB publishes the only broad same-GPU throughput cohort found in this review: on one RTX 5090, across all 1,651 OmniDocBench v1.6 pages, it reports 1.217 pages/s for MiniCPM-OCR 1.0, 1.179 for GLM-OCR, 0.818 for PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, 0.724 for Xiaomi-OCR-0, 0.416 for HunyuanOCR-1.5, 0.141 for TeleOCR, and 0.063 for DeepSeek-OCR-2. This is useful pre-test evidence that these systems can run on a 5090 in the authors’ selected configurations. It is a vendor-reported cohort, not a fully reproducible apples-to-apples benchmark: each uses its official SDK and acceleration/defaults, region-level concurrency can differ, and OpenBMB says exact versions, full evaluation scripts and timing boundaries remain to be added. Precision, peak VRAM, and the per-model runtime configuration are not reported in the comparison. [OpenBMB throughput results](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-OCR/blob/main/docs/minicpm_ocr_1dot0_en.md) · [reproduction notes](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-OCR/blob/main/docs/benchmark_reproduction_en.md).

For initial 5090 evaluation, **MiniCPM-OCR 1.0, OvisOCR3, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, Xiaomi-OCR-0, GLM-OCR, TeleOCR, and dots.mocr** provide a useful quality / speed / pipeline contrast. Add **HunyuanOCR-1.5** as a multilingual probe and **Youtu-Parsing-Omni** as a technical-graphics candidate only if their custom license terms fit the intended use. **Chandra OCR 2** is a public quality reference, but its modified OpenRAIL-M weight license imposes use restrictions; do not download or run it for eOCR until the intended use is checked against those terms. **Qianfan-OCR** remains a structured-output probe. **Jina-OCR-v1** is a performance-oriented reference with published A100/L4 results, but its CC BY-NC license excludes commercial use. **DeepSeek-OCR-2** is a slower baseline. This shortlist is constrained by the limited Russian and Chinese-script evidence described below; it is not a recommendation to ship any model.

## What the scores do and do not say

### Document parsing benchmarks

The current OmniDocBench repository describes v1.6 as a 1,651-page full-page parsing benchmark. Its Overall is the arithmetic mean of text accuracy `(1 − Text Edit Distance) × 100`, Formula CDM, and Table TEDS. Reading-order Edit Distance is reported separately and excluded from Overall. Thus one high Overall does not guarantee good reading order, and does not test editable DOCX, source-vector preservation, signature handling, or Word equation objects. The v1.6 update added 296 harder pages, including nested tables, dense formulas and unconventional layouts. The repository’s last visible leaderboard update is 2026-09-11; later values below are model-author reports, even when they use v1.6. [OmniDocBench scoring, version notes and leaderboard](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench).

Real5-OmniDocBench is a benchmark-owner leaderboard evaluation (not an independent lab run) of the same 1,355 OmniDocBench v1.5 source pages reconstructed under five acquisition conditions—scanning, warping, screen photography, illumination and skew—for 6,775 images. Its leaderboard aggregates all five conditions and reports per-condition scores. The owner added MiniCPM-OCR 1.0 and OvisOCR3 on 2026-11-02. [Real5 dataset and current leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PaddlePaddle/Real5-OmniDocBench).

PureDocBench is a separate, source-traceable challenge: 1,475 pages rendered from HTML/CSS, with matched clean, digitally degraded and real-degraded images (4,425 total), and source-derived text, formula, table and reading-order annotations. Its paper argues that OmniDocBench’s >90 scores are saturated and reports a best three-track average of 79.69/100 across 58 models. A published PureDoc score is not interchangeable with OmniDocBench Overall. [PureDocBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07492) · [benchmark-owner results](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zhihengli-casia/puredocbench).

An additional current-record watchlist item is **OXR-1.0**: the OmniDocBench maintainer table lists 97.03 (the submitter’s issue requested 97.1832 after a complete v1.6 run; the maintainer had not yet confirmed that submission in the issue thread). The Real5 mismatch is material: MiniCPM’s card reports 93.68 versus the current owner row 93.75; OvisOCR3 reports 93.63 versus owner 93.71; Xiaomi’s card reports 95.24 versus owner 92.73. Prefer the benchmark-owner table for the current cross-model Real5 comparison, while retaining each author claim as a separate result. The tables do not identify one universal winner.

### Multilingual and general OCR benchmarks

MDPBench should be a required language gate. It contains 3,400 images across 17 languages and both digital-born and photographed documents, with public/private splits and separate per-language results. The inventory explicitly includes **Russian, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese**. The paper reports average open-model drops of 17.8% on photographs and 14.0% on non-Latin scripts. The current benchmark-owner leaderboard gives the following snapshot for eOCR-relevant candidates:

| Model | Overall | EN | RU | Traditional Chinese |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dots.mocr | 80.5 | 87.4 | 71.2 | 79.6 |
| Chandra OCR 2 | 79.7 | 86.5 | 74.0 | 76.3 |
| PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 | 78.9 | 83.5 | 75.1 | 76.7 |
| HunyuanOCR-1.5 | 76.8 | 80.4 | 67.4 | 77.2 |

These are MDPBench leaderboard scores, not CER and not local eOCR measurements. The aggregate scores are close, while the target-language results trade off: dots.mocr leads this group on the listed English and Traditional Chinese rows, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 on Russian, and Chandra OCR 2 is close on English. This supports testing more than one alternative; it does not establish one candidate as universally better. The current owner table is the comparison snapshot; older paper tables and model-author summaries should remain separately labeled rather than merged into it. [MDPBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28130) · [current owner leaderboard and language breakdown](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Delores-Lin/MDPBench).

Older MDPBench Table 3 text-edit results give PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 RU 0.033 / Traditional Chinese 0.065 and GLM-OCR RU 0.064 / Traditional Chinese 0.076 (lower is better). They are useful language baselines, not results for the newest models. OvisOCR3’s card reports MDPBench 88.80, but its result does not appear on the current benchmark-owner leaderboard and the card does not give the needed RU / Traditional Chinese breakdown. MiniCPM-OCR 1.0, Xiaomi-OCR-0 and Youtu-Parsing-Omni likewise have no current owner-published MDPBench row found in this review. Do not treat language tags or aggregate MDP scores as proof of Russian/Hant accuracy.

A reproducible user report on PaddleOCR’s issue tracker describes 1.6 Cyrillic substitutions on public born-digital Russian/Kazakh PDFs while 1.5 extracted their native text correctly under the reporter’s pinned Docker/runtime settings on an RTX 3090. It is not an independent lab study and does not cancel the MDPBench result; it makes Russian a must-test risk for 1.6, especially for PDFs with a usable text layer. [Issue #18088](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/issues/18088).

OCRBench v2 is a complementary **bilingual** text-localization and reasoning test: 10,000 human-verified question/answer pairs over 31 scenarios and 23 tasks, plus a 1,500-image private test. It is not a multilingual document-parsing benchmark and should not be used to infer RU or Traditional Chinese quality. OpenBMB reports 54.7 for MiniCPM-OCR 1.0; that is its own run. [NeurIPS 2025 benchmark paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/8c2e6bb15be1894b8fb4e0f9bcad1739-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html).

olmOCR-Bench uses page-to-Markdown output checked against unit tests rather than a single reference transcript. Its release has 1,403 pages and about 7,010 checks for required/forbidden text, reading order, table structure and other properties. Jina reports 83.4 and MiniCPM reports 82.7, but these author evaluations should be read under their respective documented rendering, generation and scoring setups. [Official olmOCR benchmark](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr/blob/main/olmocr/bench/README.md) · [MiniCPM scoring notes](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-OCR/blob/main/docs/benchmark_reproduction_en.md).

## 5090 throughput and deployment evidence

OpenBMB’s cohort used the complete 1,651-image OmniDocBench v1.6 set, RTX 5090, image-level concurrency 1, and each system’s official SDK with acceleration enabled and other settings at defaults. Multiple regions inside the active page can still be recognized concurrently. Thus these figures are vendor self-reports of *system pipelines* and should be treated as a helpful same-host cohort, not an independent reproduction or proof of model-only speed. No public row reports peak VRAM or a common precision/backend. [Full cohort and latency values](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-OCR/blob/main/docs/minicpm_ocr_1dot0_en.md) · [limitations of reproduction notes](https://github.com/OpenBMB/MiniCPM-OCR/blob/main/docs/benchmark_reproduction_en.md).

| System / pipeline | pages/s | P95 sec/page | RTX 5090 evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| MiniCPM-OCR 1.0 + PP-DocLayoutV3 + SDK | 1.217 | 2.1 | Cohort |
| GLM-OCR + layout pipeline | 1.179 | 2.0 | Cohort |
| PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 + layout pipeline | 0.818 | 2.8 | Cohort |
| Xiaomi-OCR-0 + PP-DocLayoutV3 pipeline | 0.724 | 4.0 | Cohort |
| HunyuanOCR-1.5 | 0.416 | 7.2 | Cohort |
| TeleOCR | 0.141 | 13.2 | Cohort |
| DeepSeek-OCR-2 | 0.063 | 22.5 | Cohort |

MiniCPM’s guide reports 2.133 pages/s at image-level concurrency 8 on the same 5090. That is a different throughput setting. It also lists BF16 weights and official llama.cpp/GGUF, AWQ, GPTQ and DSpark variants; one community GGUF page reports a single RTX 5090 32GB setup at context size 40,960 using about 26GB VRAM, but this is a separate configuration and is not the cohort’s memory measurement. [OpenBMB model card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM-OCR-1.0) · [GGUF page](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM-OCR-1.0-gguf).

Other published speed numbers are not comparable to the cohort: Jina-OCR-v1 reports 2.57 pages/s on an A100 SXM4 40GB at concurrency 32 over olmOCR-Bench’s 1,403 pages, and L4 decoding-token comparisons; Qianfan reports 1.024 pages/s on one A100 with W8A8 quantization; HunyuanOCR-1.5 reports 2.14× DFlash-over-autoregressive decoding in vLLM but does not identify the accelerator in the relevant table. None establishes RTX 5090 latency or peak VRAM. [Jina card](https://huggingface.co/jinaai/jina-ocr-v1) · [Qianfan card](https://huggingface.co/baidu/Qianfan-OCR) · [Hunyuan paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04884).

For models with published parameter count and BF16 weights, raw weight storage is roughly 2 bytes/parameter (for example, 5B ≈10GB before activations, KV cache, runtime buffers and any companion layout model). This arithmetic is a storage estimate, **not** measured peak VRAM or proof of a usable 5090 configuration. The 5090 cohort is the only sourced full-pipeline evidence here; for OvisOCR3, Youtu, Jina, Qianfan and OXR-1.0, 5090 feasibility remains unverified.

## Technical graphics, signatures, formulas and the DOCX target

Several models return LaTeX for formulas and HTML/Markdown for tables, which gives the conversion layer structured material to transform into editable Word equations and tables. This is not evidence that any model emits Office Math/OMML or a finished editable DOCX. The public benchmarks score serialized page text, tables, formulas and reading order—not whether a Word document opens with native equation objects or preserves styles.

Paddle, MiniCPM, Xiaomi and Hunyuan list seal recognition; Ovis lists figure captioning/chart parsing and Youtu lists chart, flowchart and geometry parsing. **A seal is not a handwritten signature.** No candidate’s cited card/paper demonstrated robust signature detection with raster placement in a DOCX. Likewise, page-image OCR cannot by itself preserve vector source objects from the PDF. Source-object retention should be handled and evaluated in a PDF extraction layer; the OCR model should supply reading order, text and semantic region output.

## Closed/hosted quality references

Keep these outside a local-GPU comparison:

- **Gemini 3 Pro** is a closed-model reference. It scores 92.91 in the currently published OmniDocBench v1.6 table, 89.24 on the current Real5 owner board, and 86.4 on MDPBench’s public overall table. The protocols differ, and open-weight models can score higher on particular boards; this is a quality reference, not an upper bound in every measure. [OmniDocBench](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench) · [Real5](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PaddlePaddle/Real5-OmniDocBench) · [MDPBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Delores-Lin/MDPBench).
- **OCRia-1** is a 4B hosted/service model with no public local weights or deployment code located in this review. Its technical report reports ParseBench 87.4/100 (the authors’ benchmark), olmOCR-Bench 83.5, and about 4,700 output tokens/s on one B200 using a block-diffusion speculator, quantization and custom kernels. Those are vendor self-reports and cannot be mapped to RTX 5090 performance or the open local candidate cohort. [Technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00092) · [LlamaIndex product/research post](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/parse-ocria-1).

## Follow-up evidence gaps

Before selecting a system, a later controlled evaluation should pin weight revisions, layout dependencies, runtime/backend, precision and generation caps; measure full-PDF wall time, P50/P95/P99, peak VRAM and failure rate on the host 5090; and retain per-page prediction files for the same official scorers. The first subset should deliberately include Russian and Kazakh born-digital PDFs, Traditional and Simplified Chinese, formulas, technical charts/diagrams, and photographed/degraded pages. Evaluate editable OMML output, vector reuse, signature insertion and round-trip DOCX fidelity separately because the cited OCR benchmarks do not cover them.

For future Rust/Lean work, public weights do not imply an auditable or easy-to-port inference path. The candidate stacks include custom vLLM kernels/plugins (MiniCPM/DSpark, Jina/FastMTP, Hunyuan/DFlash, Youtu), external layout models, and SDK post-processing. No cited project provides Lean proofs of full inference or GPU kernels. Record the exact code, kernels, licenses and runtime dependency boundary during the eventual prototype comparison.
